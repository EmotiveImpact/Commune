Recommended designs

One synthesised design per screen. Built from the parts of the variants that proved out (from an IA + UX perspective) and tuned for what feels native on iOS — familiar patterns, single primary action per view, scannable rows, and temporal grouping for time-based content.

1 · Notifications

Synthesis: N1's date-grouped inbox as the spine, with N3's type-coded icon tints (bill red · chore sage · people blue · system grey) so users can still sub-scan by type without an extra filter UI. Unread rows get a subtle sage-tinted fill (not noisy). Swipe to dismiss per iOS convention. "Mark all read" as the header's right action.

2 · Settle up

Synthesis: S1's net-balance ink hero + per-person rows as the spine. When the graph can be simplified, S2's smart-suggest card appears as a dismissable "one tap to clear" helper just under the hero. Two clear sections: "Owes you" (Remind, ghost button) and "You owe" (Pay, sage primary). Amounts colour-coded inline. Rename from Command Centre.

3 · Analytics

Synthesis: A1's donut as the hero moment + two tight KPIs from A2 (Total + Biggest — not four, don't split attention). A 6-month trend bar anchors context. Below, top categories as the same row style we use elsewhere so the page feels continuous with the rest of the app. Drops A3's heatmap — novel, but low signal on quiet months.

4 · Recurring expenses

Synthesis: R1's "next billing" ink hero gives you the single number you care about (what's next, when, how much). Below, a slim version of R2's horizontal date strip shows upcoming billing days at a glance — tap a date to jump to those items. Full list is date-grouped so cadence is implicit. Drops R3 (cadence grouping is intellectually neat but low-value in practice).

5 · Activity feed

Synthesis: V1's chronological date-grouped rows (human sentences, not audit-log shorthand) + a simple 2-pill segmented control borrowed from V2 — just All / You, not three filters. Avatars used when it's an action by a person (instead of category icon), which is more social and closer to Venmo's feel. Drops V3 (too cold for a consumer app).
