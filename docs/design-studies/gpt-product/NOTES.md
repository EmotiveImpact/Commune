# GPT product study notes

## What I kept

- The settled Commune palette: sage `#2d6a4f`, navy `#1a1e2e`, cream `#f5f3ef`, and muted `#888880`.
- The warm editorial voice and the established DM Serif Display / DM Sans type pairing from the pitch deck.
- The strongest existing messages: “Run your shared space without the awkwardness”, “Live clearly, together”, and the focus on knowing what is owed and what needs attention.
- The four real product pillars: communal finance, space hubs, responsibilities, and the cross-group command centre.
- The current use cases: house shares, couples, studios/workspaces, group trips, and creative projects.
- The current monthly prices and plan limits: Standard £4.99, Pro £9.99, and Agency £29.99.
- Existing product language and states from the app, including Outstanding, Coming your way, Cross-group netting, Operations, Add expense, and the Foundry House test fixture.

## What I changed

- Put the software in the hero instead of relying on a lifestyle-photo carousel. The existing landing has a strong mood but delays the clearest proof of what Commune actually does.
- Made the app preview interactive across Command Centre, Money, and Operations so the finance and responsibilities story is visible in one coherent surface.
- Reworked the page hierarchy around the two questions in the product vision: “What do I owe?” and “What needs attention right now?”
- Reduced the feature presentation to four high-signal rows, matching the established pillars instead of adding more feature cards.
- Kept the warm editorial character through large serif statements, asymmetric layouts, generous space, and restrained colour blocks, while making the product UI denser and more practical.
- Removed invented adoption metrics and avatar-based social proof. The page now makes only claims supported by the repository.
- Used values and labels already present in repository fixtures and templates for the static product composition: Foundry House, Internet at £80, Kitchen reset, Bins out, and House admin check.

## What still does not work

- This is a static study, not the production app. The preview controls switch views and chores can be checked locally, but the actions do not persist or connect to Commune data.
- DM Serif Display and DM Sans are loaded from Google Fonts. The file still opens offline with system fallbacks, but exact typography requires a network connection unless the font files are bundled later.
- The production web navigation is broad. This study intentionally shows only the few destinations needed to explain the product, so it should not be treated as a full information-architecture proposal.
- The existing product uses both “chores” and “operations” in different surfaces. This study leads with “Operations” and uses chore language only in explanatory copy; the product should settle that terminology consistently.
- Pricing plan feature copy is retained from the current landing and pitch deck. It should be checked against live entitlement enforcement before a production landing ships.
